The thesis route

From a first question to your own interpretation

Ohtia follows the stages of a thesis, A to G. Start at any stage with the work you already have, and return to earlier decisions whenever the research asks for it.

  1. AOnboardingYour profile and a useful first focus
  2. BTopic and problemCompared topics, question and objectives
  3. CLiteratureSearch strategy, examined sources and framework
  4. DMethodologyA justified, feasible design
  5. EInvestigateCorpus, execution records and coverage
  6. FDescribeObservations linked to objectives
  7. GInterpretYour arguments, contribution and limits

Principles

Built so the research stays yours

You are the author

Ohtia asks questions, summarizes sources and criticizes with evidence. It does not write your thesis for you.

Projects stay on your computer

Sources, notes, drafts and history live in local files. You never need an Ohtia account.

Evidence you can trace

Claims keep their source, the version examined and a precise location. Evidence, interpretation and inference stay distinct.

Your judgment decides

AI proposals stay separate from what you have reviewed and confirmed. Uncertainty and gaps in coverage stay visible.

Bring your own AI

Sign in with ChatGPT or add an Anthropic or OpenAI API key. Ohtia shows you what each request sends.

Research that lasts

Versions, provenance and decisions are kept, and outputs stay useful beyond any particular model or subscription.

Questions

Before you start

Does Ohtia write my thesis?

No. You write the thesis or paper. Ohtia’s agents ask questions, summarize sources and point out weak evidence. Any change they propose to your text waits for your approval and keeps its attribution.

Where is my research stored?

On your computer. Ohtia does not need an account. When you use an AI or search connection, the request goes to that service, and Ohtia shows you what it sends before it does.

Which AI services can I use?

Version 0.2.0 supports signing in with ChatGPT and Anthropic or OpenAI API keys. Sign-ins and keys are kept in your operating system’s credential store. More services will be added through adapters.

Is it free?

Ohtia is open source under the MIT license. The AI service you connect bills you through your own plan or API key.

Can I help?

Yes. Read the contributing guide on GitHub. Help with translations and reviewing linguistic quality is especially welcome.
